Local SEO

Local SEO Strategies
to Outrank Competitors.

By John Youngblood Nov 3, 2026 4 min read

Effective local SEO strategies make your business easier to find, evaluate, and contact when nearby customers need your services. The foundation is accurate business information, useful website content, genuine reviews, and relevant local visibility.

This guide explains how to prioritize those elements, identify competitive gaps, and measure progress without relying on ranking guarantees.

Ranking Factors

What determines your position in local search?

Google says local results primarily depend on relevance, distance, and prominence: how closely your business matches a search, how far it is from the searcher, and how well-known it is. Complete business information helps establish relevance, while links and reviews contribute to prominence. You can't control a customer's location, so focus on the information and reputation you can improve. (Google)

Start your competitive assessment with a manageable set of searches covering your main services and actual service locations. Review both map results and website listings, recording the businesses that repeatedly appear. Treat this as a planning exercise rather than a definitive ranking report, since distance affects local results. (Google)

Create a comparison sheet covering profile completeness, service coverage, review themes, website usefulness, and contact options. Look for questions competitors leave unanswered, not just keywords they repeat.

Google Business Profile

Strengthen your Google Business Profile.

Google Business Profile is the current name for Google My Business. Use it as your business's public information hub, with details that accurately reflect how customers can interact with you. (OSU Extension)

Work through this checklist:

01

Business name

Use your real-world name, without adding promotional phrases or unnecessary location keywords.

02

Categories

Choose the most specific category that represents your core business, and add only genuinely relevant categories.

03

Contact details

Check your phone number, website destination, address where appropriate, and operating hours.

04

Customer context

Add accurate services, useful photos, and applicable business attributes. (Google)

Service-area businesses: hide the address.

If you travel to customers and don't serve them at your business address, hide that address and specify your service area. Don't present a home office as a customer-facing storefront when it isn't one. Google's guidance treats service-area businesses differently from businesses that receive customers on-site. (Google)

Your Website

Build service pages that help customers decide.

Plan your website around the services customers need rather than an exhaustive list of keyword variations. For each important service, explain the problem you solve, the work involved, relevant limitations, and how to request help. Use service and location language naturally where it clarifies the page.

Give location pages a distinct purpose

Before creating a city page, identify what makes it useful to someone in that location. Consider adding genuine project examples, accurate coverage information, or locally relevant service considerations. Avoid near-identical pages that only swap city names: pages created to capture similar searches and funnel visitors elsewhere can count as doorway abuse under Google's spam policies. (Google Search Central)

Make the next step obvious

Place a clear contact option alongside the information needed to make a decision. Test your phone links and inquiry form on a mobile device, and ask someone unfamiliar with your business to complete an inquiry. Use any confusion they run into to simplify the page before adding more content.

Citations

Approach citation building as an accuracy project.

Citation building means establishing mentions of your business information on other websites, often in directories or industry listings. Google draws business information from multiple sources, including publicly available web content, so accuracy is a sensible priority. (Google)

Use a controlled process rather than submitting to every directory available:

01

Create a master record

Document your business name, public contact details, website, and service description.

02

Audit existing listings

Identify outdated phone numbers, old websites, duplicate entries, and incorrect operating details.

03

Prioritize relevant opportunities

Consider established industry directories, local business organizations, and legitimate supplier directories.

04

Keep a maintenance log

Record listing access details, and review the information whenever your business changes.

Treat listing volume as an activity count, not proof of improved rankings. Evaluate each opportunity for accuracy, relevance, and usefulness to potential customers.

Reviews & Relationships

Earn reviews and local relationships honestly.

Google states that review quantity and positive ratings can help local rankings. (Google) Ask customers for honest feedback through a direct review link or QR code, and respond thoughtfully. Don't offer discounts, gifts, or other incentives in exchange for reviews; Google prohibits that practice. (Google)

Alongside review requests, identify real community relationships worth developing. Consider contributing expertise to a local organization or supplying useful project information to an industry publication. Pursue relationships for their genuine value rather than buying links to manipulate rankings, which violates Google's spam policies. (Google Search Central)

For Contractors

How should contractors prioritize local SEO work?

Contractor local SEO strategies should start with accurate service-area information, clear service descriptions, and credible evidence of completed work. Build the plan around the jobs you actually want and the locations you can reliably serve. Keep the difference between visiting customers and receiving them at your premises explicit. (Google)

For example, a roofing contractor might prioritize separate explanations of repair and replacement, supported by genuine project photos and an estimate-request process. Publish licensing, insurance, warranty, or emergency availability claims only when you can back them up. Give customers enough detail to understand whether their project fits before they inquire.

Measurement

Measure qualified inquiries and keep improving.

Review Business Profile interactions alongside your own inquiry records. Google's call metric counts call-button clicks, not confirmed conversations, while website clicks count visits started from the profile link. Neither metric alone establishes lead quality. (Google)

Set a monthly review routine: check business information, examine inquiries, identify unanswered customer questions, and choose the next improvement. Prioritize work that removes confusion or helps customers take action.
